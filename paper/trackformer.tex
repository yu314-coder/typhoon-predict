\begin{abstract}
Trackformer 1.2 advances an atmospheric basin state and a translating pressure
core to forecast tropical-cyclone motion and central pressure. Recurrent
convolutional networks evolve the fields; multiscale attention supplies
environmental context. The reported centre is associated with a depression in
the predicted core, and central pressure is sampled from that same field.
Twenty six-hour transitions produce a five-day forecast. This paper describes
only the released architecture, input contract, update equations, objectives
and ensemble mechanism. It contains no benchmark comparisons or skill claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Design and forecast state}
The system is not a route predictor followed by an independently drawn pressure
image. It evolves pressure and atmospheric states, proposes a translation of a
storm-following frame, then associates a centre with the predicted pressure
within that frame. Translation is learned and steering-conditioned: this is
neither a pressure-only tracker nor a complete atmospheric numerical solver.

At step $k$, the recurrent state is
\[
s_k=(g_k,h^g_k,c_k,h^c_k,a_k,o_k,z_k,m_k,v_k;G,R,I,M).
\]
Here $g$ is the normalized eight-channel basin field; $h^g$ its memory;
$c$ the core pressure; $h^c$ core memory; $a$ its anomaly relative to the basin;
$o$ the core-grid origin; $z$ the associated storm centre; $m$ displacement per
six hours; and $v$ auxiliary maximum wind. Static geography $G$, regional
coordinates $R$, issue intensity $I$ and masks $M$ stay fixed.

\section{Inputs, geometry and outputs}
Nine past analyses at $-48,-42,\ldots,0$ h form a tensor of shape
$B\times9\times8\times25\times33$. The basin spans $60$ to $0^\circ$N and
$100$ to $180^\circ$E at $2.5^\circ$ spacing. Channels are
\[
[\mathrm{MSLP},Z_{500},u_{850},v_{850},u_{500},v_{500},u_{200},v_{200}],
\]
in hPa, metres and metres per second. Channel normalization is
$\widetilde x_j=(x_j-\mu_j)/\sigma_j$, with the released training-only constants.
Statistics are not refitted for each forecast.

Optional regional pressure history has shape $B\times9\times1\times121\times121$
on an issue-anchored patch sampled every $0.25^\circ$, with offsets of
$\pm15^\circ$. A detail-availability flag chooses native regional history or
coarse basin pressure during initialization. The regional coordinate grid is
still supplied when native observations are unavailable.

Four static channels encode latitude/$90$, longitude/$180-1$, land fraction
and elevation/$8000$ m. The issue state supplies latitude/longitude, east/north
motion in km per six hours, maximum wind in knots, central pressure in hPa and
two intensity-availability masks. Only observations and analyses at or before
issue time may enter inference; retrospective data availability is a separate
operational question.

The internal core is a $65\times65$ grid at 20-km sampling over $\pm640$ km.
Its field is a learned reconstruction, not native 20-km observations.
For each of twenty leads ($6,12,\ldots,120$ h), outputs include basin fields,
regional pressure, core pressure/coordinates, storm centre, central pressure,
domain masks and auxiliary maximum wind. This implementation does not output
quadrant wind radii or a resolved surface-wind map.

\section{Neural modules}
The released non-tiny architecture has 21,452,595 trainable parameters.
\subsection{Multiresolution evolution networks}
The basin network receives 44 channels: eight fields, 32 memory features and
four static channels. Encoder widths are $72,144,288,432$; residual-block depths
are $2,2,4,4$. A $3\times3$ stem and stride-two convolutions produce four
scales. The decoder uses $1\times1$ projections, bilinear upsampling, additive
skip connections and one residual block at each reconstructed scale.
Output heads emit ten transition channels and 32 recurrent features. Eight
transition channels are field tendencies and two are flow corrections.

Each residual block comprises a depthwise $5\times5$ convolution, eight-group
normalization, $1\times1$ expansion by four, GELU and $1\times1$ contraction.
A learned residual multiplier starts at 0.01. Transition heads start at zero.
The core network uses widths $64,128,256,384$ and the same depths. Its 45 inputs
are core pressure, eight environmental fields, 32 core-memory channels and four
geographic channels. Three outputs represent one anomaly tendency and two
local-flow corrections.

\subsection{Convolutional recurrent memory}
Basin and core memories each have 32 channels. History encoders receive 72
flattened basin channels or nine pressure-history channels, respectively.
Given advected memory $h$ and new evolution features $f$,
\begin{align*}
(r,u)&=\operatorname{sigmoid}(W_g*[h,f]),\\
q&=\tanh(W_q*[r\odot h,f]),\\
h'&=(1-u)\odot h+u\odot q.
\end{align*}
The gate convolution emits 64 channels split into reset and update gates;
the candidate convolution emits 32. Forecast leads share these recurrent
modules rather than using twenty independent predictors.

\subsection{Environmental attention}
A $1\times1$ convolution projects $[g,h^g,G]$ from 44 to 64 channels.
Average pooling at sizes/strides three and five with ceiling mode yields
$9\times11+5\times7=134$ environmental tokens. Two pre-normalization
Transformer layers use width 64, four heads, feed-forward width 128,
ReLU activation and zero dropout. Each of the 825 basin cells supplies a
query projected from its memory to width 64. Four-head cross-attention
retrieves context; a zero-initialized $64\to32$ projection adds it to basin
memory before the transition. No separate positional embedding is added:
geographic coordinates are already among the token inputs.

\section{Observation-conditioned initialization}
Write $\sample(F,\xi)$ for bilinear field sampling. Sampling uses FP32 geometry,
aligned corners and border padding for the basin; transported core anomalies
use zero padding. All nine pressure histories are sampled onto the core grid
centred at the issue observation. A detail mask chooses native pressure or
coarse pressure. The latest map initializes $c_0$, while all nine maps initialize
the core memory.

If issue central pressure is valid, the initial core is corrected by
\[
c_0(x,y)\leftarrow c_0(x,y)+
(\widetilde p_{\rm issue}-c_0(0,0))
e^{-(x^2+y^2)/(2\,100^2)}T(x,y),
\]
where $x,y$ are kilometres and
$T=\operatorname{clip}((640-\sqrt{x^2+y^2})/160,0,1)$.
The anomaly is $a_0=c_0-\sample(g_{0,\mathrm{MSLP}},\xi_0)$.
This compact prior is applied only at initialization. Future scalar pressure
is not repeatedly inserted into the field.

\section{One six-hour transition}
\subsection{Basin transport}
Learned softmax weights blend physical winds at 850, 500 and 200 hPa.
Their initialization is $(0.4,0.4,0.2)$, not a fixed inference constraint.
For network output $d$,
\[
U_k=\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell(u_\ell,v_\ell)_k+15\tanh(d_{8:10})
\quad\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
\]
Backtracing constructs departure coordinates $D(U_k)$ and
\[
g_{k+1}=\sample(g_k,D(U_k))+0.2\tanh(d_{0:8}).
\]
Six hours convert 1 m/s to 21.6 km. Coordinate conversion uses 111.2 km per
latitude degree and $111.2\max(\cos\phi,0.2)$ km per longitude degree.
The attention-conditioned memory is advected by the same departure grid and
updated with new features. Tendencies are in normalized channel units.
This semi-Lagrangian-style update is not a complete or exactly conservative
atmospheric equation solver.

\subsection{Moving-frame proposal}
A $44\to64\to3$ GELU MLP reads sampled 32-channel memory, eight basin fields,
previous motion divided by 100, and two issue-intensity masks. For output $b$,
\[
m_{k+1}=0.7(21.6)U_k(z_k)+0.3m_k+100\tanh(b_{0:2}).
\]
This displacement, in km per six hours, defines a new origin relative to
$z_k$. It proposes where the pressure core moves, but is not the final reported
centre. The third output updates auxiliary wind:
$v_{k+1}=\max(0,v_k+5b_2)$.

\subsection{Relative core transport}
The core network reads the previous pressure core, its old sampled environment,
core memory and geography at the proposed origin. Local velocity is
$U^{\rm local}=\sample(U_k)+5\tanh(d^c_{1:3})$.
Subtracting $m_{k+1}/21.6$ removes bulk frame translation. Backward coordinates
also account for the offset between the previous associated centre and its
previous grid origin. On this relative departure grid $D_c$,
\begin{align*}
a_{k+1}&=\sample(a_k,D_c)+0.1d^c_0,\\
c_{k+1}&=\sample(g_{k+1,\mathrm{MSLP}},\xi_{k+1})+Ta_{k+1}.
\end{align*}
Unlike the basin tendency, $d^c_0$ is not bounded by $\tanh$.
Core memory uses the same relative sampling followed by its gated update.

\subsection{Centre association and central pressure}
Convert core pressure to hPa: $P=\sigma_p c_{k+1}+\mu_p$.
For cells within 300 km of the proposed origin,
\[
w_i=\operatorname{softmax}_i\left(-P_i/2-r_i^2/(2\,180^2)\right).
\]
Cells beyond 300 km are excluded. The weighted mean of cell latitude/longitude
is $z_{k+1}$. Central pressure is the bilinear sample $\sample(P,z_{k+1})$,
not necessarily the grid minimum. The spatial prior associates the forecast
with this storm rather than an unrelated remote depression.

\subsection{Geographic pressure output}
The issue-anchored regional output is
\[
\widehat p_R=\sample(g_{k+1,\mathrm{MSLP}},R)
             +\sample(Ta_{k+1},R;\mathrm{zero\ padding}),
\]
then de-normalized to hPa. This basin-plus-core reconstruction is generated by
the model itself; future observed fields and display-only vortices are not
substituted. Its $0.25^\circ$ sampling is not proof of native resolution.
Masks indicate valid basin coverage. Resampling and localized association mean
the reported centre, central pressure and regional map minimum need not coincide
exactly. A display must preserve those distinctions rather than relocate fields.

\section{Autoregression and learning objectives}
Inference initializes once and repeats the transition twenty times, using only
fixed issue context and its own predicted future state. The implemented
single-step loss is
\begin{align*}
L={}&L_{\rm env}+L_{\rm regional}+0.2L_{\rm route}
    +0.5L_{\rm pressure}+0.05L_{\rm wind}\\
 &+0.25L_{\Delta p}+0.1L_{\Delta z}.
\end{align*}
The final terms require valid preceding-target labels. Environment loss is
cosine-latitude-weighted Smooth L1 in normalized units. Regional loss includes
Smooth L1 and $0.2$ times adjacent-grid gradient errors on each axis. Absolute
regional-field supervision excludes the inner 150 km around the target centre,
because reanalysis can underresolve observed central pressure. This is a
supervision choice, not proof of correct inner-core structure.

Route residuals use a 100-km scale; pressure and wind use 20 hPa and 30 kt.
Pressure-change error uses 10 hPa and displacement-change error uses 100 km.
All objectives use explicit masks. They train field evolution and readout
together, without adding an independent central-pressure output head.
Training-only normalization, storm-level split boundaries and causal input
selection remain essential implementation constraints.

\section{Single forecasts and 50-member means}
Evaluation-mode inference is deterministic for fixed inputs and weights.
The documented ensemble perturbs only normalized historical weather: independent
Gaussian fields are spatially smoothed with kernels five (basin) and nine
(regional), centred, unit-scaled per map and multiplied by amplitudes 0.025 and
0.015. Issue centre, motion, intensity, masks and geography remain fixed.
Each member performs a complete recurrent rollout.

With $M=50$, routes and central pressures are averaged separately:
$\overline z=M^{-1}\sum_m z^{(m)}$ and $\overline p=M^{-1}\sum_m p^{(m)}$.
Physical pressure fields are averaged on common geographic grids, not by
averaging unaligned moving-core array indices. In general,
\[
\overline z\ne\arg\min_x\overline P(x),\qquad
\overline p\ne\min_x\overline P(x).
\]
These are perturbed-input realizations of one model, not 50 trained networks or
learned latent samples. Spread is not automatically calibrated uncertainty.
Member counts, perturbation settings and seed policy must accompany the outputs.

\section{Source identity and use boundary}
The public name is \textbf{Trackformer 1.2}. Its manifest retains the internal
checkpoint identity, 1.2.73 epoch 4, and exact weight/source hashes.
\texttt{model.py} implements environmental attention and added temporal losses;
\texttt{baseline\_model.py} implements moving-core evolution and readout;
\texttt{v165\_base.py} supplies sampling, evolution blocks and memories.
All are in \texttt{models/trackformer\_1\_2\_field/}.

This paper describes mechanisms only. Learned reconstructions, limited domain,
input-source differences and retrospective availability constrain interpretation.
The model remains a research artifact, not an operational warning system.
